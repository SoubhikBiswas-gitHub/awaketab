title: 1.0 — launch
date: 2026-09-26
---

AwakeTab 1.0 keeps your screen on from a browser tab, and the status pill only says "Screen awake" when the browser has actually granted the lock. Nothing to install, no account, no cookies. It is free, and there are no ads on the awake screen.

On the awake screen: press M for Clock, Focus (25/5-minute blocks, with today's count), Minimal, Night, Message (Pro) or Cook. Cook mode has tap-to-pause and three named kitchen timers. A session can end with a soft chime, an optional notification and a flashing tab title, then offers +15, +30 or +60 minutes. Stats shows today (minutes and sessions), this week, your streak, a 12-week heatmap and a CSV export. The floating window has Stop and +15. With Pro it also shows the clock or your focus block. The pop-up version opens in your language. The installed app works offline and offers updates only when no session is running.

In your language: the tool is available in eight languages. The ten most-read guides are now also in Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, German, French, Japanese, Simplified Chinese and Hindi, marked "native review pending" until a native speaker has checked them. Pages in other languages keep their address when the tool starts.

Steadier and faster: the page no longer moves when a session starts, and the first screen appears sooner on phones. Keyboard focus is visible everywhere, every journey works with the keyboard alone, and D steps through every theme.

Beyond the tab: AwakeTab for Chrome keeps the display, or only the computer, awake while the tab is hidden, using Chrome's own power API. It has a badge, Alt+Shift+A, and, with Pro, schedules and auto-start. Store links appear on the /extension page once the listing is approved. Sites can add a "keep my screen on" widget with one line of code (see /embed). Lobby screens can use kiosk start links (see /kiosk). The open-source lock library behind AwakeTab is ready for npm (see /library).

Pro and licences: cancelling a yearly plan keeps Pro until the end of the period you paid for. Removing a device on /pro/manage takes effect on that device. Moving your key into the extension uses one activation, not two. Lifetime licences no longer ask for the key again after 90 days.

Still honest: a web page cannot keep the screen on while its tab is hidden, cannot keep Teams or Slack "Available", and cannot stop sleep when you close a laptop lid. AwakeTab says so, and it never fakes mouse or keyboard input. The real-device results on How we tested are pending until the manual run is recorded.
